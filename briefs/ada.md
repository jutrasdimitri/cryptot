# Brief — Cardano (ADA): The Slow, Peer-Reviewed Tortoise

*Knowledge brief for The Crypto Historian. History and concepts only. No current prices — real-time data comes from the live feed, never from this document.*

## The birth: an Ethereum co-founder walks away and starts over

Cardano begins with a departure. **Charles Hoskinson**, a Colorado-born mathematician, was one of Ethereum's co-founders and briefly its chief executive — until mid-2014, when a dispute over direction (he wanted a for-profit structure with venture funding; Vitalik Buterin wanted a non-profit foundation) ended with Hoskinson leaving. In **2015**, he founded the engineering company **IOHK** with **Jeremy Wood**, and a Japanese group — the network of partners that became **Emurgo** — hired IOHK to build something audacious: a blockchain designed the way academia designs things, with peer-reviewed research papers published *before* the code, not after.

The approach was unusual in every way, including the fundraising. Instead of a splashy Western ICO, ADA was sold through a **voucher sale held almost entirely in Japan and Asia, from September 2015 to January 2017**, priced in yen and settled in bitcoin, raising about **62 million dollars**. The great majority of buyers — often estimated around 90% — were Japanese, which is why early Cardano was nicknamed **"the Japanese Ethereum"** and why Japan still has a deep Cardano community today. There were no U.S. participants, a deliberate choice that kept the sale outside U.S. securities rules at a time when almost nobody else was thinking about that.

The network itself launched on **September 29, 2017**, and ADA began trading in early October. The names tell you about the founders' tastes: Cardano honors **Gerolamo Cardano**, a 16th-century Italian mathematician; ADA honors **Ada Lovelace**, the 19th-century mathematician often called the first computer programmer. Even the development eras are named for poets and thinkers — Byron, Shelley, Goguen, Basho, Voltaire.

## The long road: eras, mockery, and receipts

Cardano was built in public, in stages, and that slowness became its defining drama. The **Byron** era (2017) was the basic chain: ADA could move, nothing more. **Shelley**, launched **July 29, 2020**, turned on staking — ADA holders could delegate their coins to community-run stake pools through a proof-of-stake system called **Ouroboros**, the first consensus protocol of its kind to be built from peer-reviewed research and formally proven secure. **Goguen**, crowned by the **Alonzo** upgrade on **September 12, 2021**, finally brought smart contracts. Later upgrades — **Vasil** in September 2022 — made the chain faster and cheaper to build on, and on **September 1, 2024**, the **Chang** hard fork switched on full on-chain governance, moving Cardano into its **Voltaire** era: the treasury and the rules now belong to a vote of the ADA holders themselves.

Through the long building years, critics coined a nickname that stuck like glue: **"ghost chain"** — the accusation that Cardano was all research and ceremony, with no real users haunting it. The Historian treats this joke the way a good teacher treats all good jokes: there's a kernel of truth in it, and he says so honestly. The milestones genuinely were late, and for years you could not do much on Cardano beyond send and stake. The community's counter-argument is also a matter of record: era by era, the promised things did eventually ship — staking, smart contracts, governance — each one peer-reviewed like a bridge built slowly by engineers who publish their calculations. Whether the tortoise's pace is wisdom or a fatal flaw is, honestly, the central debate of Cardano's whole history — and it's still being argued today.

Meanwhile the chain quietly collected real-world chapters. In **2021**, Cardano partnered with Ethiopia's Ministry of Education to build blockchain-based identity and academic records for millions of students and teachers. And on **September 30, 2026**, Brazil's state energy giant **Petrobras** and the **Cardano Foundation** announced two working applications built with PUC-Rio's Ledger Labs: one tokenizes the environmental attributes of sustainable aviation fuel (as **CS-SAF** tokens, so the same green credit can never be claimed twice), and a second tracks Petrobras' renewable **Diesel R** through its lifecycle. Research-stage pilots, not mass deployment — but the kind of unglamorous, real-economy use the project always said it was built for.

## What Cardano is trying to be

A blockchain built like infrastructure rather than like an app: formally verified where it counts, energy-light by design (proof of stake from research, not as an afterthought), capped forever at **45 billion ADA**, and governed by its holders. Its bet is that the systems the world will trust with identity, credentials, and public records will be the ones built slowly and proven on paper first.

## Three stories to tell

1. **The sale nobody in America attended.** A top-ten blockchain funded almost entirely by Japanese retail buyers, in yen, over sixteen months — with buyers reportedly so careful that the sale team built education and compliance programs around it. Cardano's Japanese roots still show: a loyal community, and ADA eventually listing on Japanese exchanges years after the rest of the world.
2. **The "ghost chain" roast — and the receipts.** For years the joke wrote itself: conferences, papers, no products. Then the eras landed one after another — Shelley, Alonzo, Chang — each late, each real. The community learned to answer the joke with a shrug and a timeline. The Historian's honest line: *the ghost was slow — but it kept showing up.*
3. **The oil giant and the green fuel.** In 2026, Petrobras — one of the largest energy companies on Earth — chose Cardano to make its sustainable-fuel claims auditable, so no airline can claim the same green credit twice. It's a very Cardano story: not a casino, not a meme — a ledger for receipts.

## Sensitive topics — never state without a live source

- The "~90% Japanese buyers" figure is a widely repeated estimate, not an audited number — phrase it that way.
- The Petrobras projects are **research-stage pilots** with no disclosed commercial volumes — never present them as mass adoption or live at scale.
- Staking reward rates change over time — never quote a percentage from memory.
- Hoskinson's personal claims and controversies (his biography is sometimes disputed by journalists) — stick to the documented project history, not the personality wars.
- Ethiopia: describe the 2021 partnership announcement accurately; do not claim current deployment scale without a fresh source.
