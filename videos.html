What Is Leverage? Borrowing to Bet Bigger, Explained

What is leverage, in plain language? Borrowing money to control a bigger position than your own cash allows. With $100 and 10x leverage you control a $1,000 position — but $900 of it is borrowed. A 10% move against you can wipe out your $100, and the exchange can force-close your position: that forced closing is a liquidation. Spot buying — owning the actual coin, like Solana, with no borrowed money — has no forced closing hanging over it.

What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging? Small Steps, Steady Plan

Dollar-cost averaging, in plain language: instead of trying to pick one perfect day to buy, you buy a set dollar amount on a set schedule — for example $50 every payday — no matter what the price is doing. Lower prices buy more Bitcoin with the same dollars; higher prices buy fewer. Over time your average cost lands in the middle. It does not remove risk and does not guarantee a profit.

What Is Staking? Your Coins Stay in Your Wallet

Some crypto networks do not use miners: coin holders help secure the network by staking — pledging their coins behind honest record-keeping — and the network pays rewards in return. Cardano works this way: you delegate your ADA to a stake pool, and your coins never leave your wallet. Rewards are never guaranteed, and staked coins still rise and fall with the market.
